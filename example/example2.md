# The Industrial Revolution

## Origins

The %[Industrial Revolution](Technology) began in Britain in the late 18th century and
spread across Europe and North America during the 19th century. It marked a fundamental
shift from hand production to machine-based manufacturing.

{#industrial-revolution-origins}
The transition was driven by a combination of factors: available coal and iron, a culture
of practical invention, and access to global trade routes. %[James Watt](Persons) and his
improved %[steam engine](Technology|Steam engine) are often cited as the central catalyst.

## Key Inventions

%[Richard Arkwright](Persons) developed the water frame in 1769, enabling mechanised
cotton spinning. This was one of the first examples of factory-based %[textile production](Technology).

%[Isambard Kingdom Brunel](Persons) later pushed engineering further, designing railways,
bridges and steamships that connected industrialised regions. His %[steamships](Technology|Steam engine)
demonstrated that %[steam power](Technology|Steam engine) could cross oceans reliably.

## Social Impact

The revolution transformed society as much as industry. Workers moved from rural areas
into growing cities. New social classes emerged alongside new forms of labour. The changes
laid the groundwork for the modern world.
