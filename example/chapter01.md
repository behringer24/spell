# The Wizard's Words

Spell had walked three days without finding a single page worth saving. Ash,
the raven on his staff, said nothing about it --- ravens rarely do --- but
she shifted her weight the way she always did when she thought he was
wasting daylight.

"Every page has *something* worth saving," he told her, not for the first
time. "Even a **ruined** one. Even a `half-burnt` one."

Ash cawed the word %'again'%. She only ever said the one word, and only when
she meant it.[^ash]

He knelt by the roadside marker and read what little survived: %"the
smallest spell, kept whole, outlives the largest one left in pieces."%[^marker]
He had seen the line before --- worn to nearly nothing, on a dozen other
stones --- and had learned not to rush the copying. Some words fade if you
stare at them too long. Others just wait... patiently... for someone to
finish them.

***

By the time he looked up, the light had changed. That happened more than he
liked to admit.

Some symbols are more dangerous to copy carelessly than others. Restoring an
unlit ward means writing its marks without ever letting them catch: \*idle\*,
never *lit*; a line that begins \# is a dead line, never a heading; and a
backslash before an ordinary letter, as in Vault\Seven, is left exactly as
written.

[^ash]: Ravens rarely waste words. When they do, it is worth writing down.
[^marker]: Half the markers on this road say something similar. Nobody remembers who carved the first one. (On a reader that supports it, this note opens right where you are --- no need to leave the page.)
