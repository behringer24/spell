# The Age of Bright Engines

## Origins

The %[Age of Bright Engines](Inventions) began, by the chronicler's own count,
three generations before the fall of the old archive, and lasted less than
one lifetime after it. It marked a shift from candlelight and memory to
something steadier: light that could be stored, carried, and --- when
broken --- restored.

{#bright-engines-origins}
The shift was driven by one stubborn insight: that light, properly bound,
need not gutter the way flame does. %[Maer Thallow](Persons) is credited
with the first working %[everlight lantern](Inventions|Everlight lantern),
built from little more than glass, patience, and forty failed attempts.

## Notable Works

%[Perrin Vask](Persons) followed a few years later with %[lightweave](Inventions),
a cloth woven to hold a faint glow of its own. It was one of the first
signs that Thallow's trick could be put to work at scale, not just kept in
a jar on a shelf.

%[Corvin Ashgrave](Persons) pushed the craft further still, lining ships'
hulls with %[everlight](Inventions|Everlight lantern) so they could hold a
course after dark. His %[lit-ships](Inventions|Everlight lantern) were said
to cross open water that no captain would risk by starlight alone.

## What It Left Behind

The age changed more than its engines. Villages that once closed at dusk
kept working past it. New trades opened around the making and mending of
lanterns. Whether any of it made people happier is a question the
chronicle, wisely, does not try to answer.
